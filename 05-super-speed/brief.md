# Give Farlight a clear route back to offers

Draft for Helen Achebe, Director of Product, and the Dispatch product management team. Decision requested: approve the proposed recovery policy and experience below for an initial Farlight pilot. Thresholds, response times, and pilot limits below are author recommendations, not existing Rook policy or evidence-derived values. Engineering must confirm the required historical records and phone confirmation capability before implementation. No release date is proposed.

## The problem for one responder

Farlight received 12 offers and took eight in August 3–9. In release week she received ten and took four. That fell to three offers with none taken in August 17–23, one with none taken the following week, and zero in August 31–September 6 in the Module 3 extract. Her handler reported checking that she was available, yet could not explain why her phone stayed quiet. On September 1, the handler passed on her words: “nothing again this week. starting to wonder if im still even in the system.” [1, 2]

The code lowers the same acceptance-history score for a miss and an explicit decline. Only accepting another offer raises it; waiting does not. Someone ahead of Farlight can accept before the list reaches her. This provides a plausible explanation for her loss of opportunities, while historical ranking and eligibility evidence is still needed to confirm it. [3]

## What changes for Farlight and her handler

Farlight’s coverage card shows the last four weeks of offers, taken offers, misses and explicit declines, her stated availability, and her last offer. “Review my offers” opens a request for her handler; it remains available even when records are incomplete. One active request per responder prevents duplicate work. The handler sees who owns the request, its next update date, and a plain-English reason at every stage.

Nadia’s team owns the review and responds within two working days. Marcus owns technical investigations. A request qualifies for a score correction when the previous seven days contain no offers, at least three callouts for which Farlight was available in the correct region with every required capability, and a reproducible ranking history showing that earlier misses reduced her opportunities. Handlers may request investigation earlier, but a falling offer count alone does not authorize correction. Use complete, comparable availability periods when interpreting that decline.

For this pilot, propose two linked changes: correct supported historical miss penalties and stop subtracting points for new misses while Farlight participates. Continue to record misses, investigate delivery problems, and preserve explicit-decline penalties and acceptance credits. This tests whether she can sustain access to suitable offers without repeated corrections. Keeping the decline penalty is a limited-scope assumption, not a finding that all declines should reduce future opportunities; Helen should commission that policy review separately.

The historical correction covers misses from the verified August 12 rollout time through the correction cutoff. If rollout time cannot be established, start August 13. Replay every event from a verified starting score through the current event, applying the original floor and ceiling after each event and excluding only those selected miss penalties. Earlier misses and every explicit decline remain. Establish the diagnosis by replaying historical callouts with actual ranking inputs: at least one suitable, available callout must place Farlight before the responder who actually accepted when the selected miss penalties are removed. This demonstrates an opportunity to receive an offer, not that she would have accepted. If earlier attempts would also have changed, flag the replay as uncertain and withhold correction pending further evidence. Nadia approves the evidence and Marcus approves the technical replay; a restricted engineering operation applies it after confirmation. No recurring reset is included.

Farlight receives an in-app message: “Our review found that missed offers pushed you below someone who accepted a suitable callout. We can correct those miss penalties and try a different rule for future misses. Would you like to take part?” Choices are “Confirm” and “Not now.” Explain the 14-day pilot, that work is not guaranteed, that deliberate declines still affect the score, and how to leave the pilot. Confirmation expires after seven days; “Not now” leaves the request paused, and the handler can ask to resume it. Her availability stays under her or her handler’s control. The message is only shown after the evidence and approvals above are complete.

After the correction succeeds, her handler sees “History corrected — watching for suitable offers.” Farlight sees the same outcome in her app. The next offer follows the existing order calculation and 60-second wait, using the corrected score. The card records whether it was taken, declined, or missed. Monitoring runs for the entire pilot, including after her first acceptance; a correction or one acceptance alone never closes the case as recovered.

If the review finds no suitable callouts, show “No suitable callouts found during this period,” with the dates checked. If she was unavailable, show the recorded periods and a link to availability controls. If evidence is missing, show “We need more information,” assign Marcus, and give the next update date. Historical silence is never labeled a refusal. If the cause is confirmed as something else, Nadia records that cause and the responsible team before closing the score-review request.

If Farlight still receives no offer after three further eligible callouts while available, or seven calendar days after correction, reopen the technical investigation automatically. Tell both Farlight and her handler that the review is continuing and when to expect an update. Marcus checks delivery, eligibility and ranking with a response due within two working days. A newly missed offer also triggers this review. Support cannot repeatedly reset scores or promise a priority offer.

## What it will not do

- Guarantee work or place Farlight ahead of responders regardless of capabilities and travel time.
- Change her stated availability or the shared availability record that Supply reads for maintenance planning.
- Automatically reset every quiet responder’s score, remove deliberate-decline penalties, restore the 90-second wait, or reverse all of 4.2.
- Treat silence as a deliberate refusal or claim that seasonality has been ruled out.

## How Helen can judge it

Start with Farlight for 14 calendar days after correction. The first offer and acceptance establish initial recovery. Review the full pilot for sustained opportunity: report eligible callouts, offers reaching her, acceptances, misses, declines, and the longest eligible-callout sequence without an offer. Require an offer in each half of the pilot that contains at least three eligible callouts while she is available, no unresolved escalation, and no repeated correction. Also require that the handler can follow the case without another ticket. If either half has fewer than three eligible callouts, report insufficient opportunity to evaluate sustained recovery. A single case establishes feasibility, not the causal impact of the policy.

These thresholds are explicit trial assumptions. Seven days provides a weekly checkpoint, three eligible callouts separates continued silence from no suitable demand, and 14 days permits two observations. No source establishes them as optimal. Ravi will report actual counts, availability periods and review workload so Helen can revise them before a larger trial. Remove the three-day percentage trigger from automated scope until a reliable availability-adjusted baseline exists; handlers can still request a review at any time.

Ravi reports offer receipt and acceptance, assignment share across all callouts, time to assignment, and any unsuitable or duplicate assignment. Compare the operational measures with the preceding 14 days, describing demand differences. Any unsuitable or duplicate assignment pauses expansion immediately. A five-percentage-point fall in assignment share or a 20% rise in median time to assignment triggers Helen’s review before expansion; these are provisional review triggers, not statistical proof. Marcus can stop new corrections while investigating; existing corrections are not silently reversed. Helen owns the decision to expand after reviewing demand and the small sample. [3–5]

Also report who loses first offers when Farlight moves up, and changes in their offer counts and travel times, in an internal aggregate view. Nadia records minutes spent on the case and escalation count. During this one-person pilot, accept one active recovery case and no expansion if the two-working-day update commitment cannot be met. An update may explain an ongoing investigation; it is not a promise of resolution.

At day 14, Nadia gives Farlight and her handler the results and a dated next step. Helen chooses whether to extend, run a larger controlled trial, or stop. If no decision is ready, keep the approved pilot rule for this existing participant and pause enrollment, with weekly updates until a decision. Withdrawal or an explicit stop ends prospective miss protection at a recorded time without charging earlier misses retroactively. Existing approved corrections remain unless a separate audited reversal is justified. No single-person result authorizes a general rollout.

## Build acceptance criteria

- Given an authorized handler, the card shows Farlight’s dated counts and last offer. A week with zero offers displays zero; unavailable data displays “Unknown.” Other responders’ private histories are not exposed.
- Given repeated request submissions, only one active review exists. Only Farlight’s signed-in responder account can confirm. Handlers and Support cannot confirm on her behalf.
- Given verified starting history, explicit declines and acceptance credits remain in the replay. An approved correction only removes miss penalties within the approved interval, applies bounds after every event, stays within 0–1, and never lowers the score. If history or historical ranking evidence is insufficient, correction is blocked with a named investigation owner.
- Given expired confirmation, withdrawn consent, or an approval more than seven days old, no correction runs. Obtain fresh approval and confirmation. Recheck both immediately before applying it.
- Given live scoring events during correction, preserve every newer event. Use one durable correction identifier so retries cannot apply it twice. A failed write leaves the request in “Correction pending” and alerts Marcus; success is shown only after the write is confirmed.
- Given a committed correction, retain before/after score, replay interval, excluded miss events, evidence, approval, confirmation, operator and time. Restrict access to the assigned Support and Engineering roles under existing access controls. The audit record must support reconstruction and an explicitly reviewed reversal.
- Given the first successful write, follow-up counts only callouts occurring afterward. Three consecutive eligible callouts without an offer, seven days without an offer, or a new miss creates one escalation, with owner and next update visible. An acceptance records initial recovery; monitoring and the case remain open through the full pilot and its outcome review.
- Given an active pilot, a miss is logged without subtracting score, while a deliberate decline retains its existing penalty. Activating the rule and the historical correction must take effect together at the recorded cutoff. If either fails, neither is presented as complete. Ending participation applies the agreed prospective rule from its recorded end time without retroactive penalties.
- Given a justified reversal, rebuild from the last verified starting state using all later real events and the approved correction history. Never overwrite the live score with the old saved value. Marcus authorizes the reconstructed change and Nadia explains its effect to Farlight and her handler.
- Given deployment, availability values and the shared record read by Supply remain unchanged. Before build, inspect Security Policy 4.1 and confirm existing account access, retention and audit requirements for these responder records. Use cover identity only. No legal-identity mapping is required.

Engineering prerequisite: establish whether event history, starting scores, historical availability, required capabilities and ranking inputs are retained and reliable. If they are missing, implement prospective evidence capture and the review states first; do not infer historical corrections from weekly totals. Sofia owns the card and app copy, Nadia owns review guidance, Marcus owns safe correction and monitoring, and Helen approves the recovery policy before launch.

The sample `history.py` stores scores in an in-memory dictionary and does not establish durable storage, locking or replay. Marcus must identify the production owner and persistence of scores. The design must serialize corrections with live outcome events per responder or retry against a changed version, retaining newer events and correction identifiers through restarts. Confirm that the phone app supports authenticated confirmation, expiry and status updates; if it requires a mobile release, include that work explicitly and defer the recovery flow until it is available. No handler confirmation substitute is assumed.
